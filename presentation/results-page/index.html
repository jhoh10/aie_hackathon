How this was run, and what it leaves out
Cases
20 PRs on PocketBase (MIT): 8 real upstream fixes, 4 real fixes reverted under a "no behaviour change" description, and 8 bad PRs written by a separate agent that never saw the verifier. 8 development cases, 12 held out.
Stopped
CI or the implementer's tests failing, review requesting changes, or the verifier blocking or sending to a human.
Verifier
Writes checks from approved claims without seeing the code, runs them on old and new versions in a sealed sandbox, and compares probes near the diff. "Claims only" ignores the probes.
Models
Claude subagents wrote claims, tests, probes, reviews and the implementer's tests. Meta's model wasn't available yet.
Tuning
After the development run, undeclared changes rated minor (such as a reworded error) became a note instead of a reason to ask a person. A rule for unstable probes was tried and dropped. One held-out verdict was seen before this change; all verdicts are recomputed from recorded evidence.
Approval
In the benchmark nobody edited the claims before approving them. In real use a person would, which could prevent some wrong blocks but not all of them.
Left out
Probe and review answers were written in batches, so writers saw related cases. That can flatter both. A sample this small shows direction, not significance.
